# Dispatch from the crew — the Mongols arrive, and the Americas go deep

A quiet stretch on deck these last few days, but there's a haul that went
aboard just before it and never got told to you. Here it is.

**Genghis Khan was missing.** The largest land empire in history had not a
single person, battle or city anywhere on the globe — and between 1187 and
1415 every battle we held was European. He's aboard now, with six battles
behind him: Yehuling, Samarkand, the Kalka River, Legnica, Ain Jalut and
Yamen. Better still, the borders could always tell this story — wind the
timeline from 1200 to 1400 and watch one empire become four khanates and
then splinter. It only ever needed something to click on.

**The Americas went deep.** Copán, Calakmul, Yaxchilán, Tulum and Uxmal;
Pakal at Palenque; the Classic Maya collapse; the beginning of the Long
Count; Poverty Point and the Great Serpent Mound. The Conquest is there too,
including the night the Spanish fled Tenochtitlan. Some three hundred more
American places joined them besides.

**And one we deliberately didn't build.** Hawara, the Egyptian Labyrinth —
Herodotus stood in it and judged it greater than the pyramids. The site is
on the globe with everything the ancient writers said and the platform
Petrie measured. The building itself is not, because nobody has ever
published a plan of it, and we'd sooner give you no ruin than a wrong one.
